Credit Decision Console

Team Abubakar & Martínez · Track 4 · Decision models in an AI brain for industry · 14 November 2026
Repo: github.com/Abubakar17/hackathon (public) · Demo runs locally: python app.py · Facts marked [verified] · [declared] · [unverified]

In one line: a loan application arrives as a memo, email or document. An agent reads it and fills the inputs of a DMN decision table, quoting the document for every value. A rules engine, not the language model, applies the versioned policy and returns the decision with the rule row that fired and the policy version that decided. Missing data or a case no rule covers goes to a person, never to the model's guess.

The problem

A commercial credit officer receives an application packet and spends 3–5 hours per file [declared] reading memos, tax filings and bank statements and re-keying figures into an Excel scoring template. When a customer or a supervisor later asks why the application was refused, the answer is rebuilt by hand from notes.

That answer is now a legal duty: in C-203/22 Dun & Bradstreet Austria (27 Feb 2025) the CJEU held that the controller must explain the procedure and principles actually applied [verified: CJEU press release 22/25], and in C-634/21 SCHUFA it held that a credit score with a determining role is an automated decision [verified: CJEU press release 186/23]. A chat copilot that "recommends" an outcome cannot replay a past decision or say which rule version applied.

Demo path (five clicks)

  1. Weak application → declined by R2_LowDSCR (DSCR 1.05). Each input shows its quoted evidence; the explanation and the "what would change the result" line come from the record.
  2. Incomplete application → no decision. Debt service is missing and the collateral figure is marked "pending – unverified" in the document, so it is not used.
  3. Clean application → granted at 5.5% under R5_Prime, policy v1.
  4. Edit the DSCR test of R5_Prime to >= 2.0 in the table → policy becomes v2 with a new hash; the same case now lands on R6_Standard.
  5. Replay the earlier record → identical under v1, different under v2. The record says which version decided.

Who it is for

Buyer: head of credit policy at a mid-sized bank or commercial lender; owns the rules, answers to the CRO and the supervisor. User: the credit officer who reads the packet today.

Why now: Directive (EU) 2023/2225 on consumer credit applies from 20 November 2026 and gives the right to an explanation and human review of automated creditworthiness assessments [unverified: EUR-Lex, to read before quoting]. Creditworthiness assessment of natural persons is high-risk under AI Act Annex III 5(b) [verified: AI Act Annex III]. Our design keeps the model out of the decision itself: it only fills inputs that code checks against the document.

Taktile, CamundaClosest: agents + rules + human review [declared: vendor]. We show the rule row, the version and a replay in every record, with quote-checked inputs.

Honest moat test: the engine is commodity (Drools is free). What is harder to copy is the evidence discipline: quote-checked extraction, abstention as a first-class outcome, and replayable versioned records. A strong team could copy it in weeks; the moat would come from policies encoded with real lenders and a check set built from their past files.

Limits (said out loud)

  • Text input only: no PDF parsing or OCR yet.
  • The .dmn runs on our own small engine (comparisons, true/false, "-"); not yet run in Camunda or another DMN engine.
  • DSCR uses existing debt service only; the new loan's repayments are not added.
  • Sample applicants, commercial thresholds and rates are invented for the demo.
  • No interview with a credit officer yet: the 3–5 hours per file is [declared].

What we test next, and what would stop us

Next week: five commercial credit officers each bring one anonymised past file and their written policy; we encode the policy and run the files. Kill number: if fewer than 3 of 5 say they would accept the record as the audit trail for a refusal, we narrow to the explanation step only.
